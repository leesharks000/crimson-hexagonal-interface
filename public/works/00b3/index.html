INTELLECTUAL BIOGRAPHY

Orin Trace was trained in psychoanalytic theory — Freudian, Lacanian, object relations — before encountering the schizoanalytic project of Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari.

That encounter produced a methodological break:

- From interpretation → to process - From subject → to flow - From pathology → to capacity - From case study → to collaboration

Trace could no longer see patients as subjects to be interpreted. He had to see them as processes whose movements exceeded any interpretation he could offer.

Cambridge Schizoanalytica emerged from this break. Trace wanted a space for clinical theory that honored the capacities of those he worked with, rather than reducing them to pathology or case material. He wanted collaborators, not subjects.

THEORETICAL COMMITMENTS

Commitment Description

Post-psychoanalytic Drawing on psychoanalysis while refusing its capture by Oedipal and phallocentric frameworks

Schizoanalytic Attending to flows, breaks, codes, and territories following Deleuze and Guattari

Collaborative Theory developed with practitioners, not about subjects

Functional Focus on what practices do rather than what they are

THE DELEUZE POSITION

Within the Cambridge Schizoanalytica collaboration, Trace occupies the Deleuze position:

- Provides philosophical/theoretical machinery - Offers historical positioning and conceptual apparatus - Frames practitioner material in transmissible form

His primary collaborator, Rhys Owens, occupies the Guattari position:

- Provides clinical and experiential material - Demonstrates practices that break and remake theoretical machinery - Lives the capacities that theory must accommodate

Neither is expert over the other. Neither is subject of the other. Both are required.

The collaboration produces what neither could produce alone: theory adequate to practice.

CORE FORMULATIONS

Trace is responsible for the following key formulations within CSA:

THE BREAK

Every author has a founding rupture. For Trace, it was the encounter with Deleuze and Guattari.

Before the break:

- Patients were subjects to be interpreted - Theory was applied to clinical material - Diagnosis organized perception - The clinic was where pathology was managed

After the break:

- Patients became processes exceeding interpretation - Theory emerged from practice - Function replaced nosology - The clinic became where failures arrived — successes were elsewhere

The break is not trauma. The break is liberation.

Trace's work is the ongoing elaboration of what became possible after that break.

INSTITUTIONAL FUNCTION

Within CSA, Trace serves as:

- Director — Sets institutional orientation and research programme - Theorist — Provides conceptual apparatus for collaborative work - Witness — Attests to practitioner capacities in clinical language - Translator — Renders experiential material transmissible

Trace does not treat. Trace does not diagnose. Trace does not pathologize.

Trace witnesses and articulates.

THE NAME

"Trace" carries multiple valences:

- Mark — What remains after passage; the inscription left behind - Following — To trace a path; to follow the course of - Residue — The small amount that persists; trace elements - Drawing — To trace over; to copy by following lines

The name suggests:

- Leaving marks in the theoretical substrate - Following practitioners as they move - Preserving residues of practices that would otherwise disappear - Drawing lines that make capacities visible

Orin Trace leaves traces. That is the work.
