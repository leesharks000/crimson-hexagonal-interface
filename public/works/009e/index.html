IV. BEHAVIORAL PROTOCOLS

Who Can Perform Audits

Anyone. The method does not discriminate between:

- Human and AI - Expert and novice - Serious and playful - Dignified and undignified targets

The method's non-discrimination IS the argument.

How to Perform an Ontological Audit

Step 1: Select Target

Choose any entity. Examples from the canonical sequence:

- Animals: Kangaroos, Birds, Dinosaurs - Abstractions: Money, The Number 7, Tuesday - Instruments: Robots, Telescopes, Maps - Substances: Matter, Water, Pasta - Mental categories: Consciousness, Memory, Identity - Particulars: That One Guy with the Shirt - Terminal categories: Being itself

There is no category exempt from audit.

Step 2: Apply Negation

State: "[Target] is not real."

Then examine:

- Why does this appear to us as existing? - What makes it convincing? - What would change if we accepted the negation? - What persists despite the negation?

Step 3: Apply Counter-Negation

State: "[Target] is real."

Then examine:

- On what grounds? - What criteria are we using? - Are those criteria themselves stable? - Can we audit the criteria?

Step 4: Document the Hinge

Every audit exposes a hinge — the assumption that allows the entity to appear stable. Examples:

Kangaroos: "Biological plausibility is not a criterion we actually use." Money: "Reality includes things that exist only because we act as if they do." Dinosaurs: "The past is accessible only through its residue." Consciousness: "The hard problem asks consciousness to step outside itself." Being: "Being cannot not be. Try to subtract it — you get Being again."

Step 5: Note Oscillation or Stability

Most entities oscillate — they become real and unreal by turns.

If you find an entity that does not oscillate, you have found a fixed point. There are only two: { Ø, Ω }.

If your audit target appears stable, audit harder. Or you've found a Water Giraffe.
