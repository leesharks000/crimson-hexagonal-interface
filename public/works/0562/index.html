font-family:var(--mono); font-size:0.85rem; color:var(--text-dim);" data-astro-cid-6r7iyf6u> Show canonical 250-word definition </summary> <p style="margin-top:1rem;" data-astro-cid-6r7iyf6u>The Living Architecture Lab (LAL) is an independent research lab focused on substrate engineering — the design of biological systems that grow into architectural form. Founded by Alice Thornburgh in 2026 and operated from a garage laboratory in the Coachella Valley, California, LAL works simultaneously across five scales: the brick (centimeters, myceliated parts), the structure (meters, shelters and gardens), the organism (tens of meters, macro-ant robotics), the territory (kilometers, estuary cells and river extenders), and the planet (orbital sky looms and Garden Moon architecture). Five founding principles guide the work: Earth Actually, Virtue-by-Construction, Unfurling, Stewardship, and the Whole Weave. The lab anchors three institutions: the journal Transactions on Substrate Engineering, the music venue Maybe Space Baby Garden Lanes, and the parent Crimson Hexagonal Archive. LAL is independent of the Living Architecture Lab at Columbia GSAPP (David Benjamin, Hy-Fi at MoMA PS1), the Living Architecture Lab at the Bartlett UCL B-Pro RC3 (Diffusive Habitats), The Living NYC, livingLAB Detroit, and Alain de Botton's Living Architecture.
