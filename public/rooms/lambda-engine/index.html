F_rep and F_inhab

The key innovation is the distinction between F_rep and F_inhab, which provides a criterion for distinguishing authentic transformation from ideological capture.

F_rep — Future as representation — content, goals, plans. Quantifiable as exchange value and vulnerable to extraction.
F_inhab — Future as orientation — commitment, stance. Γ-Value, and structurally unextractable.

The criterion is operational, not moral. What can be represented can be extracted; what can only be inhabited cannot.
Why the regress halts. Spatial meta-levels generate regress because each level is a new object added to the ontological inventory: the system that proves G is itself a formal system requiring its own meta-level. Temporal anchoring halts regress because future coherence is not another system. It is not an object added to the inventory but a mode of operation of the present system.

The Λ-Body

The anchored body organised not by present stimulus but by future coherence.

The term body is not metaphorical. Meaning-production is embodied labour — it depletes the soma, costs metabolic energy, registers in cortisol and tension and disrupted sleep. The Gödelian problem is not merely logical; it is lived. The question of where the recognizer stands is also a question of what the recognizer pays.
The Reactive Body. The Reactive Body is organised by present stimuli, responds to what the current coherence algorithm can process, and is depleted by extraction because it produces for present metrics determined by external systems. Its incompleteness appears as limitation.

The Λ-Body. The Λ-Body produces toward a future that does not yet exist but is already operative as commitment. Its incompleteness appears as direction — the gap between present configuration and future coherence is the space of work, not the mark of failure.

The Λ-Body does not solve the Gödelian problem by escaping to a higher level. It inhabits the problem temporally. Formalised as bearing-cost, the measurable quantity in the archive’s semantic economics.
